\section{Supplementary Figures}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{E1/figures/figS1_em_convergence.pdf}
\caption{EM convergence of C psmc and \psmcrs{} 0.2 on the same genomes (E1). One column per model; every replicate is a line. Iteration $i$ counts completed EM iterations: C psmc's log-likelihood of round $i+1$ and \psmcrs{}'s after $i$ iterations are both the log-likelihood after $i$ iterations. C psmc prints none after its last iteration, so both programs' log-likelihoods are drawn up to the last iteration both give. (a--d) Log-likelihood gained since the start, $\mathrm{LL}(i)-\mathrm{LL}(0)$, for both programs (C psmc wider, underneath). (e--h) \psmcrs{}'s log-likelihood minus C psmc's after the same iteration (log-likelihood units; iteration 0 evaluates the same starting model). (i--l) $\theta$ per bin of \psmcrs{} relative to C psmc's after each iteration, in percent. C psmc prints $\theta$ to six decimals, so its $\theta$ is known only to $\pm0.5\times10^{-6}$: the grey band is that rounding, relative to C psmc's smallest $\theta$ of the model at each iteration, and the steps of the lines are its quanta. The log-likelihood did not decrease between iterations in any of the \EoneRuns{} fits.}
\label{fig:s1}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{E4/figures/fig5_input_preparation.pdf}
\caption{Partly callable bins bias PSMC, and \psmcrs{}'s per-bin weighting removes most of the bias (E4). Bottleneck genomes of \EfourGenomeMb{}~Mb (\EfourRepsW{} replicates) with no missing data, or with 10\% or 30\% of positions uncallable in clustered (mean \EfourClusRunKb{}~kb) or dispersed (mean \EfourDispRunBp{}~bp) runs. Routes: \psmcrs{} on the VCF with its callable mask, each called bin weighted by its callable bases (\opt{--bin-callable auto}, the default; blue); the same bins with every called bin taken as full (\opt{--bin-callable ignore}; dark blue, dashed), which is what \texttt{fq2psmcfa} and C psmc do (the consensus FASTA $\to$ \texttt{fq2psmcfa} route gives the same bins and the same fit and is not drawn separately); multihetsep (yellow), read back with its uncalled sites placed at the start of each interval; and the VCF read without its mask (pink). (a) $\theta$ per bin relative to the complete-data fit of the same genome. (b) RMSE of $\log_{10}\Ne$ against the truth on the informative window. (c) RMSE of $\log_{10}\Ne$ against the complete-data fit, exact over log time on the window. In a--c, dots are replicates and bars their means. (d) The curves of one replicate with the 30\% dispersed mask, its complete-data fit (grey) and the truth; times outside the informative window are shaded. (e, f) Bias over time, the mean over replicates of $\log_{10}(\Ne/\Ne^{\text{true}})$, with the 30\% clustered and dispersed masks; grey, the complete data. With dispersed masks most called bins kept only part of their bases (\EfourDispTenPartly{} with 10\% missing, \EfourDispThirtyPartly{} with 30\%): taken as full, they made $\theta$ low by \EfourDispTenFqTheta{} and \EfourDispThirtyFqTheta{}, and weighted, by \EfourDispTenAutoTheta{} and \EfourDispThirtyAutoTheta{}. Simulations: $\mu=2.5\times10^{-8}$ per bp per generation, $g=25$ years.}
\label{fig:s2}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{E5/figures/fig6_bootstrap_calibration.pdf}
\caption{Calibration of the bootstrap (E5). \EfiveFits{} independent bottleneck genomes of \EfiveChroms{}$\times$50~Mb, each fitted once, give the true sampling distribution of the estimate; \EfiveDatasets{} of them are bootstrapped (\EfiveReps{} replicates) with blocks of 50~Mb (blue) and 5~Mb (dark blue, dashed). Every panel spans the model's informative window. (a) One genome: the truth (dashed), the 95\% range of the independent fits (grey), the genome's fit (blue) and the edges of its bootstrap 95\% bands. (b) Standard deviation of $\log_{10}\Ne$: the true one over the independent fits (black, with its $\chi^2$ 95\% interval, light grey) and the bootstrap's (median over the bootstrapped genomes). (c) Bootstrap SD over true SD: median over genomes and interquartile range; the light grey band is where a bootstrap whose SD equalled the true one would fall, given the true SD's own sampling error. (d) Share of the bootstrapped genomes whose 95\% band covers the true \Ne{} (thick; Clopper--Pearson 95\% interval for 50~Mb blocks) and the fits' geometric mean (thin), against the nominal 95\%; with \EfiveDatasets{} genomes the share moves in steps of 10\%. (e) The same share for each genome's pattern-projected truth, the truth averaged over each free parameter's span. One-point spikes in b--e are times at which the fits' interval boundaries, which move with each fit's $\theta$ and maximum time, straddle the grid time. The legend gives each block size's coverage averaged over the window, with intervals from resampling the bootstrapped genomes. The bootstrap's SD was a median of \EfiveFiftySd{} (50~Mb blocks) and \EfiveFiveSd{} (5~Mb) times the true one; its bands covered the true \Ne{} at only \EfiveFiftyCov{} and \EfiveFiveCov{} of times because the fits' median absolute bias, \EfiveBias{} in $\log_{10}\Ne$, was about twice their true SD, \EfiveSdTrue{}. Simulations: $\mu=2.5\times10^{-8}$ per bp per generation, $g=25$ years.}
\label{fig:s3}
\end{figure}
