\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\includegraphics[width=183mm]{E1/figures/figS1_em_convergence.pdf}}
\caption{C psmc and \psmcrs{} climb the same likelihood at every EM iteration (E1). One panel per simulated model, \EoneReps{} genomes each. Upper lines: the log-likelihood gained in iteration~$i$, $\mathrm{LL}(i)-\mathrm{LL}(i-1)$, one line per genome and program (C psmc wide and pale underneath, \psmcrs{} thin and blue on top). Grey line: the largest of the \EoneReps{} genomes' differences between the two programs' log-likelihoods after iteration~$i$. Arrows: where the two come closest, the smallest gain of any genome in that iteration divided by the largest difference, floored to two significant figures. All \EoneSoneGains{} gains were positive: the log-likelihood never decreased in either program, and in iteration~\EoneSoneLastIter{}, the last drawn, it still rose by \EoneSoneGainLastMin{} to \EoneSoneGainLastMax{} units. Before the first iteration, from the same starting model, the two log-likelihoods agreed to within \EoneSoneFirstAbsMax{}, C psmc's printed precision; after iteration~\EoneSoneLastIter{}, the last both report, \psmcrs{}'s was the higher in \EoneSoneRsHigher{} of the \EoneGenomes{} genomes. Over all iterations, $\theta$ and $\rho$ of the two programs differed by at most a relative \EoneSoneThetaMax{} and \EoneSoneRhoMax{}. Iteration~$i$ counts completed EM iterations; C psmc prints no log-likelihood after its last, so the panels end at $i=\EoneSoneLastIter{}$.}
\label{fig:s1}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\includegraphics[width=183mm]{E4/figures/fig5_input_preparation.pdf}}
\caption{Partly callable bins bias PSMC, and \psmcrs{}'s per-bin weighting removes most of the bias (E4). We read \EfourRepsW{} simulated bottleneck genomes of \EfourGenomeMb{}~Mb without missing data, or from a VCF whose callable mask leaves 10\% or 30\% of positions uncallable in clustered (mean run \EfourClusRunKb{}~kb) or dispersed (mean run \EfourDispRunBp{}~bp) runs. Blue: \psmcrs{}'s default, each called bin weighted by its callable bases (\opt{--bin-callable auto}). Orange: the bins \texttt{fq2psmcfa} makes for C psmc from the consensus sequence, which take every called bin as full; they are the VCF's bins read with \opt{--bin-callable ignore}, bin for bin, and were fitted by \psmcrs{}, which fits them as C psmc does (Fig.~1 of the article). (a) One genome with the 30\% dispersed mask, the one whose weighted fit has the median error, and the fit of the same genome without missing data (wide grey), over the model's informative window; the arrow is the factor by which taking bins as full scales $\theta$, and with it every size and time. (b) $\theta$ relative to the fit of the same genome without missing data; large circles, medians, joined by lines (offset sideways by 0.7 percentage points so that both routes show where they agree); small dots, replicates. Clustered runs empty whole bins, which both models drop. Dispersed runs leave many called bins with only part of their bases (\EfourDispTenPartly{} with 10\% missing, \EfourDispThirtyPartly{} with 30\%): taken as full, they made $\theta$ \EfourDispTenFqTheta{} and \EfourDispThirtyFqTheta{} low, against \EfourDispTenAutoTheta{} and \EfourDispThirtyAutoTheta{} weighted. Simulations: $\mu=2.5\times10^{-8}$ per bp per generation, $g=25$ years.}
\label{fig:s2}
\end{figure}

\caption{\psmcrs{}'s bootstrap measures the sampling spread of a PSMC fit, not PSMC's bias (E5). The fits of \EfiveFits{} independent bottleneck genomes of \EfiveChroms{}$\times$50~Mb give the true sampling distribution of the estimate; \EfiveDatasets{} of them were bootstrapped (\EfiveReps{} replicates) in \EfiveFiveTrunks{} blocks of 5~Mb (blue) or \EfiveFiftyTrunks{} of 50~Mb (light blue); \EfiveFiveTrunks{} blocks is about what the default 50~Mb blocks give a 3~Gb genome. (a) One bootstrapped genome: the truth (dashed), its fit and bootstrap 95\% band (\EfiveFiveTrunks{} blocks), and the 95\% range of the independent fits (grey lines), over the model's informative window; in the first interval, band and range run off the top of the panel. (b) Bootstrap SD of $\log_{10}\Ne$ (median over the bootstrapped genomes) against the true SD, the SD over the independent fits, one dot per time of the window; grey, where a bootstrap with the true SD would fall given the $\chi^2$ 95\% interval of an SD estimated from \EfiveFits{} fits; numbers, each block count's median ratio of the two SDs. (c) Share of the window's times at which a genome's band holds the mean fit (the geometric mean of the independent fits) or the truth; dots, genomes; bars, means; grey line, the nominal 95\%. The bands are nearly as wide as the fits' spread (bootstrap SD \EfiveFiveSd{} and \EfiveFiftySd{} times the true one, median over the window) but centred on a biased fit: the fits' median absolute bias, \EfiveBias{} in $\log_{10}\Ne$, was about twice their median SD, \EfiveSdTrue{}. Simulations: $\mu=2.5\times10^{-8}$ per bp per generation, $g=25$ years.}
\label{fig:s3}
\end{figure}
